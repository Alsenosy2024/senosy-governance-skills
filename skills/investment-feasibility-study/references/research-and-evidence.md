# Research and evidence protocol

## Source hierarchy

Use this order whenever it is practical:

1. Statutes, regulators, official statistics, tax/labour/municipality bodies, public procurement, and government open data.
2. Company annual reports, investor presentations, audited filings, official price lists, official menus, and contracts supplied by the user.
3. Reputable sector research with a visible methodology and date.
4. Platforms, maps, reviews, job postings, property listings, and public social content: use as dated field signal only.
5. Analogies and consultant judgment: useful for a range or hypothesis only, never a verified fact.

## Deep-research questions

Ask questions that change a decision, for example:

- Is demand large and reachable at the required price?
- Which competitors set the reference price, experience, and availability?
- Is the site/channel able to produce the required conversion and throughput?
- What regulatory, tax, labour, import, or permit constraint can delay or prevent launch?
- Which cost and volume inputs dominate NPV and cash burn?

## Dynamic-data rule

Date-stamp changing data such as rents, online ratings, menus, delivery fees, availability, platform fees, and competitor branch count. Never infer a specific live number from an undated article.

## Evidence language

- `Verified`: direct authoritative source or user-provided primary evidence.
- `Derived`: calculation from verified inputs; show formula.
- `Consultant assumption`: judgement/range; explain basis and validation action.
- `Missing`: needed but unknown; state decision consequence.

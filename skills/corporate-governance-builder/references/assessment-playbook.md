# Governance Assessment Playbook

## Assessment Logic

Do not assess governance by document existence only. Assess design, implementation, evidence, and outcomes.

Use four evidence levels:
- Level 1: Claimed verbally
- Level 2: Documented
- Level 3: Implemented with records
- Level 4: Measured and improved

## Interview Sampling

For small companies:
- Owner/founder
- CEO/general manager
- Finance
- HR/admin
- Operations
- Sales
- Procurement or person doing procurement

For medium companies:
- Add department heads
- Add selected supervisors
- Add internal audit/risk/compliance if present
- Add 2-3 employees affected by approvals and procedures

For groups/holdings:
- Holding CEO or managing partner
- Subsidiary CEOs
- Group CFO
- Group HR
- Group legal
- Shared services
- Board secretary if present
- Family office/family council if applicable

## Diagnostic Domains

### Ownership and Board

Assess:
- Formal ownership rights
- Board/advisory board role
- Meeting cadence
- Decision records
- Reserved matters
- Conflict of interest handling
- Succession and continuity

Red flags:
- Owners intervene in routine approvals without a rule.
- Board exists but has no agenda, minutes, or decision register.
- No clear separation between ownership direction and executive management.

### Executive Governance

Assess:
- CEO authority
- Executive committee role
- Management meetings
- Escalation rules
- Strategic execution review

Red flags:
- Every major issue returns to one person.
- Meetings are status conversations without decisions.
- No action tracker.

### Organization Structure

Assess:
- Reporting clarity
- Span of control
- Duplicate roles
- Missing functions
- Independence of control roles

Red flags:
- Finance reports into sales or operations in a way that weakens control.
- Procurement has no independent review.
- HR decisions happen informally with no records.

### Authority and Decision Rights

Assess:
- Approval thresholds
- Contract signing
- Payment approval
- Hiring and salary authority
- Discount and pricing authority
- Project initiation authority

Red flags:
- Authority depends on personal relationships.
- Employees ask several people until someone approves.
- Approvals happen after execution.

### Policies and Procedures

Assess:
- Policy coverage
- Approval status
- Version control
- Employee awareness
- Procedure usability
- Forms and records

Red flags:
- Policies copied from another company.
- Procedures describe ideals but not actual systems.
- No owner or review date.

### Risk, Compliance, and Controls

Assess:
- Risk identification
- Compliance obligations
- Control ownership
- Segregation of duties
- Incident reporting
- Internal audit/review

Red flags:
- Same person creates vendor, approves PO, receives goods, and approves payment.
- Conflict of interest is not declared.
- No risk acceptance authority.

### Performance and Reporting

Assess:
- KPI clarity
- Report cadence
- Data source
- Management review
- Board reporting
- Action follow-up

Red flags:
- Reports are produced but no decisions follow.
- Metrics are activity counts only.
- No targets or thresholds.

## Maturity Scoring Rubric

Score each domain from 1 to 5.

1. Ad hoc:
   - No formal structure or documents.
   - Decisions rely on individuals.
   - Evidence is mostly verbal.

2. Emerging:
   - Some documents exist.
   - Inconsistent implementation.
   - Roles and approvals unclear.

3. Defined:
   - Approved documents exist.
   - Roles and authorities are clear.
   - Core processes are documented.

4. Managed:
   - Governance is implemented.
   - Reports, minutes, registers, and controls exist.
   - Exceptions are tracked.

5. Optimized:
   - Governance is measured and improved.
   - Decisions are timely and controlled.
   - Lessons learned update the framework.

## Gap Analysis Format

Use this table:

| Domain | Current State | Target State | Gap | Risk | Priority | Recommended Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Priority logic:
- High: legal/regulatory risk, financial exposure, major decision bottleneck, fraud/control risk.
- Medium: efficiency, role clarity, reporting quality, duplicated work.
- Low: polish, formatting, non-critical maturity improvement.

## Root Cause Patterns

Common root causes:
- Founder bottleneck
- Missing authority matrix
- Structure grew organically
- Policies not connected to workflows
- Weak middle management
- No management reporting rhythm
- No document control
- Lack of independent control function
- ERP/system permissions not aligned with authority
- KPIs reward behavior that governance is trying to prevent

## Quick Wins

Typical quick wins:
- Approve interim authority matrix.
- Launch executive committee with decision/action log.
- Create document register and version control.
- Clarify top 10 recurring approvals.
- Separate payment preparation from payment approval.
- Create conflict of interest declaration form.
- Create procurement threshold rules.
- Create board/executive monthly pack.
- Create risk/issues register.

# Corporate Governance Builder

## Core Rule

Build governance as an operating system, not as documents. Always connect every deliverable to decisions, accountability, authority, controls, reporting, and measurable performance.

Use this sequence unless the user's context clearly requires a different order:

1. Scope and governance mandate
2. Input collection
3. Current-state assessment
4. Strategy and operating model alignment
5. Governance model design
6. Organization structure
7. Roles, RACI, and decision rights
8. Delegation of authority
9. Board, committees, and management forums
10. Policy architecture
11. Procedures and workflows
12. Job descriptions
13. KPIs, KRIs, and reporting
14. Risk, compliance, and internal controls
15. Implementation roadmap
16. Adoption, training, and change management
17. Audit, maturity review, and continuous improvement

## Engagement Start

Start every governance engagement by clarifying:

- Company type: private, listed, family, startup, nonprofit, government-owned, holding/group.
- Size and complexity: employees, locations, legal entities, revenue scale, regulated/unregulated.
- Trigger: growth, investor readiness, audit issue, founder bottleneck, restructuring, compliance, succession, M&A, crisis.
- Scope: whole company, group, department, board governance, authority matrix, policies only, or full operating model.
- Required outputs: slides, Word manual, Excel matrices, implementation roadmap, training pack.
- Constraints: legal jurisdiction, regulator, existing bylaws, family protocol, shareholders agreement, ERP/HR systems.

If the user asks for execution without details, assume a private medium-sized company and produce an adaptable framework with clear assumptions.

## Delivery Phases

### 1. Mandate and Scope

Define why governance is being built, who sponsors it, who approves it, and what "done" means.

Outputs:
- Governance project charter
- Stakeholder map
- Document request list
- Interview plan
- Delivery roadmap
- Assumptions and exclusions

Do not start drafting policies before the mandate is clear.

### 2. Current-State Assessment

Assess the real way decisions happen today. Review documents, interview stakeholders, map current workflows, identify gaps, and separate symptoms from root causes.

Minimum assessment lenses:
- Ownership and board effectiveness
- Executive decision-making
- Organization structure and reporting lines
- Authority and approval bottlenecks
- Policy and procedure coverage
- Risk, compliance, and controls
- People, performance, and accountability
- Management reporting and data quality
- Culture, escalation, and informal power

Outputs:
- Current-state report
- Gap analysis
- Governance maturity score
- Pain-point heatmap
- Risk/control gap list
- Quick wins

### 3. Strategy and Operating Model Alignment

Translate the company's strategy into required capabilities, decision forums, reporting lines, and controls.

Questions to answer:
- What decisions must be faster?
- What decisions must be more controlled?
- Which roles need independence?
- Which functions should be centralized or decentralized?
- Which activities require committees?
- Which metrics prove governance is working?

Outputs:
- Target operating model
- Capability map
- Decision architecture
- High-level management rhythm

### 4. Governance Model

Design the governance layers:

- Ownership layer: shareholders, partners, family council, general assembly.
- Board layer: board, advisory board, board committees.
- Executive layer: CEO, executive committee, management committees.
- Control layer: risk, compliance, internal audit, finance control, legal.
- Delivery layer: departments, PMO, process owners, service units.

Outputs:
- Governance model diagram
- Governance principles
- Decision rights model
- Escalation model
- Meeting cadence

### 5. Organization Structure

Design the structure after the governance model is understood.

Rules:
- Separate accountability from execution where risk is high.
- Avoid reporting lines that let one person request, approve, execute, and review the same transaction.
- Keep spans of control realistic.
- Make each function own a clear value stream or control purpose.
- Do not create departments only to satisfy document symmetry.

Outputs:
- Current organization chart
- Target organization chart
- Function mandate per department
- Transition structure if needed
- Role impact analysis

### 6. Roles, RACI, and Job Architecture

Define role families, accountability, handoffs, and decision participation.

Outputs:
- Role catalogue
- RACI matrix for core processes
- Job families and levels
- Job descriptions
- Critical role profiles

Use RACI carefully:
- R = does the work
- A = owns the result and final accountability
- C = consulted before decision/action
- I = informed after decision/action

Avoid multiple A owners for one activity.

### 7. Delegation of Authority

Build the authority matrix before detailed procedures. This is usually the most important practical governance artifact.

Cover at minimum:
- Strategy and budget
- Procurement and vendor selection
- Contracts and legal commitments
- Hiring, promotion, compensation, termination
- Capex and opex
- Discounts, pricing, credit, write-offs
- Bank payments and treasury
- Projects and change requests
- Exceptions and emergency approvals

Outputs:
- Delegation of authority matrix
- Approval thresholds
- Escalation rules
- Exception handling rules
- Segregation of duties checks

### 8. Committees and Forums

Create only committees that make decisions, manage risk, or coordinate cross-functional work.

For each committee define:
- Purpose
- Scope
- Chair
- Members
- Secretary
- Authority
- Inputs
- Outputs
- Meeting frequency
- Quorum
- Decision method
- Escalation path
- Standard agenda
- Minutes template

Outputs:
- Board charter if applicable
- Committee charters
- Management forum charters
- Annual governance calendar

### 9. Policy Architecture

Create a policy hierarchy before writing individual policies.

Recommended hierarchy:
- Governance manual
- Board and committee charters
- Corporate policies
- Department policies
- Procedures
- Work instructions
- Forms and templates
- Records/registers

Policy domains:
- Governance and decision-making
- Finance
- Procurement
- HR
- Legal and contracts
- Risk management
- Compliance and ethics
- Conflict of interest
- Data and information security
- Project governance
- Sales, pricing, and customer management
- Asset management

Outputs:
- Policy register
- Policy template
- Document control rules
- Approval and review cycle

### 10. Procedures and Workflows

Convert policies into executable workflows.

For each process capture:
- Trigger
- Inputs
- Steps
- Roles
- Approval points
- Controls
- Systems used
- Records generated
- SLA
- Exceptions
- KPIs

Outputs:
- Process inventory
- SIPOC or process maps
- SOPs
- Forms
- Control points
- SLA matrix

### 11. KPIs, KRIs, and Reporting

Design performance and governance reporting together.

Metrics should cover:
- Strategic outcomes
- Financial discipline
- Operational performance
- Risk exposure
- Compliance
- People and accountability
- Governance adoption

Outputs:
- KPI library
- KRI library
- Board reporting pack
- Executive dashboard
- Department scorecards
- Meeting report templates

### 12. Risk, Compliance, and Internal Control

Use COSO-style control thinking and ISO 31000-style risk thinking.

Minimum outputs:
- Risk taxonomy
- Risk register
- Control register
- Compliance obligations register
- Conflict of interest register
- Issues and actions log
- Internal audit plan or review checklist

Controls must be tied to actual processes and authority limits, not written as generic statements.

### 13. Implementation Roadmap

Prioritize by impact, risk, and adoption difficulty.

Typical waves:
- Wave 1: mandate, top structure, quick controls, authority matrix
- Wave 2: committees, policies, critical procedures, reporting
- Wave 3: job descriptions, KPIs, systems alignment, training
- Wave 4: audit, maturity review, optimization

Outputs:
- Implementation roadmap
- Workstream plan
- Change impact assessment
- Training plan
- Communications plan
- Owner/action tracker

### 14. Review and Continuous Improvement

Governance must be reviewed after use.

Review:
- Are decisions faster and clearer?
- Are exceptions decreasing?
- Are committees producing decisions?
- Are controls catching issues?
- Are managers using reports?
- Are policies followed?
- Are employees clear on authority and accountability?

Outputs:
- Governance maturity review
- Lessons learned
- Updated policy register
- Revised authority matrix
- Improvement backlog

## Quality Gates

Before finalizing, verify:

- Every major decision has an owner and approval route.
- Every committee has a reason to exist.
- Every policy has a process or control behind it.
- Every role has clear accountability.
- Every approval threshold has escalation logic.
- Risk, compliance, and internal control are embedded into workflows.
- The structure supports strategy, not personalities.
- Governance artifacts are adoptable by employees, not just impressive to consultants.

## Style for Arabic User Outputs

When producing Arabic content for the user:

- Use clear Egyptian Arabic when the user writes in Egyptian Arabic.
- Keep terms like (Governance), (RACI), (Delegation of Authority), (KPI), (KRI), (SOP), (COSO), (ISO 37000) in English inside parentheses when useful.
- Be practical and direct.
- Avoid academic padding.
- Use tables for matrices and checklists.
- Make templates ready to copy into Word, Excel, or PowerPoint.
